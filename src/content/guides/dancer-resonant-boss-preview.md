## Quick answer

The Dancer is the tentative name used in an official Xbox Wire preview for a
Resonant boss shown wielding two mallets and moving with a fast, masked,
dance-like style. Remedy explicitly warned that the name could change. The
encounter is separate from the enormous head-shaped Resonant Entity played in
the later Summer Game Fest demo, so the two previews should not be merged into
one boss page.

## What the preview actually shows

Xbox Wire describes the Dancer as a deft, masked figure carrying two mallets.
The preview focuses on the rhythm and physical intensity of the fight rather
than publishing a full move list. That supports a narrow identification guide:
the enemy is a Resonant, it is presented as a major encounter, and its working
name was Dancer at the time of the March 2026 briefing.

It does not support a final health value, arena name, elemental weakness,
phase count, reward name, or step-by-step strategy. Even the displayed name is
provisional. This page therefore keeps “Dancer” in the title players are
likely to search while preserving the evidence boundary in the answer.

## Why the fight matters to progression

Remedy says Resonants are major bosses integrated into story progression.
Defeating one grants a supernatural Combat Ability, and some Resonants can
present branching reward choices that push Dylan toward different playstyles.
That means a Resonant encounter is both a combat test and a build decision.

The public material does not say which ability or choice pool belongs to the
Dancer. Barrage, Seekers, and Shield were offered after the different Resonant
Entity in the June hands-on. Assigning those three rewards to this boss would
combine two separate demonstrations and create false information.

## How to prepare without inventing a strategy

The footage description suggests that timing and reading a mobile melee enemy
will matter more than standing at range. Dylan’s system rewards aggression:
melee hits restore Combat Ability resource, abilities can stun enemies, and
executions temporarily increase melee damage. Those global rules are useful
preparation, but they are not a proven Dancer-specific counter.

Before release, the defensible plan is to understand form switching, keep a
defensive or space-making ability available, and avoid committing to slow
animations without seeing the boss’s response. A real walkthrough will be
added only after the retail encounter can be tested for tells, punish windows,
phase transitions, checkpoint behavior, and the exact reward screen.

## Dancer versus the first hands-on Resonant

The later media demo ends with an enormous woman’s head embedded in pavement,
using telekinesis to throw rocks and debris. The Dancer is instead described
as a masked, dual-mallet combatant. They have different visual identities and
appear in different preview contexts.

Keeping them separate prevents two common errors: treating a working name as
final, and transferring mechanics or rewards from one Resonant to another.
The [first hands-on Resonant guide](/guides/first-resonant-entity-boss/)
tracks the head-shaped encounter, while the

explains the confirmed reward system without assigning it to the wrong boss.
